Mean-Reversion $5-Wide Vertical Spreads

Target: high-probability spreads closed for near-max profit within 30 days — reviewed, stress-tested, and turned into a repeatable scan.

1 — The Objective As Specified

  • $5-wide bull call or bear call / put vertical spreads
  • Cost (risk) around $2.50 per spread
  • Statistical probability of success > 90%
  • Close for 90–100% profit within 30 days
  • Entries timed by mean reversion signals

2 — The Math You Cannot Escape

Option spreads are priced so that probability and payout trade off against each other. For a $5-wide vertical, the market's own pricing implies:

Structure ($5 wide)Cost / RiskMax Return on RiskStructural POP*
At-the-money (≈50Δ short strike)~$2.50~100%~50%
30Δ short strike~$3.35 (credit ~$1.65)~49%~70%
10Δ short strike~$4.45 (credit ~$0.55)~12%~90%

*POP = probability the spread expires profitable, implied by the option chain itself.

Verdict on the literal spec: a $2.50-cost $5 spread with >90% structural POP and ~100% profit does not exist — if it did, it would be free money and arbitraged away instantly. A spread priced at $2.50 is the market saying "this is a coin flip." The 90%+ structures pay ~12%, not 100%.

But the spec is recoverable. The market prices POP off the option chain alone. It does not know you are only entering after a statistically extreme move with a trend filter. Edge comes from three places:

  1. Signal edge — short-term mean-reversion entries (RSI(2) extremes with a 200-day trend filter) have historically resolved upward in uptrending large caps far more often than 50/50. This shifts realized win rate well above structural POP.
  2. Volatility edge — panic selloffs inflate put premium exactly when you are selling it. You collect fear, then IV mean-reverts with price.
  3. Management edge — taking profit at 75–90% of max before expiry means price only has to not breach your short strike for ~2–3 weeks, not survive to expiration. This alone adds several points of realized win rate.

3 — Three Executable Profiles

All are $5-wide verticals, 30–45 DTE at entry, exited within 30 days. Realized win rate assumes the full entry checklist in §4 plus the management rules in §5.

Fable's call: run the Balanced profile as the core strategy. It is the only configuration where signal + volatility + management edge plausibly pushes realized win rate near 90% while still paying ~50% on risk. If >90% is a hard requirement, run Conservative and accept ~12% per trade (which still compounds well at 2–3 week cycle times). The Aggressive profile is playable but it is a directional trade with a coin-flip structure — size it smaller and expect drawdown streaks.

Note on debit vs. credit form

A deep in-the-money bull call debit spread and an out-of-the-money bull put credit spread at the same strikes are synthetically identical — same risk curve, same max profit/loss. Use the credit form: it typically fills closer to mid, benefits directly from the IV crush after a panic low, and the profit target is a simple buy-back order.

3.5 — Confirmation Score: Is the Long Strike Protected?

The entry checklist (§4) tells you the stock is at a statistical extreme. The Confirmation Score tells you whether there is enough additional evidence that the stock will bounce past both strikes — meaning it will close above the further-from-the-money leg (the long put in a bull put spread), the level at which your maximum loss is fully realized.

Each factor below is binary (pass/fail). The scanner calculates the score automatically. Require a score of ≥ 8 out of 12 before entering.

Factor table

FactorPtsPass conditionLogic
1. Ticker backtest win rate2 Historical 30-day positive-return rate after identical signal ≥ 80% The stock's own past behavior at this exact combination of conditions — not structural POP, but realized outcome. Calculated from up to 5 years of daily history. 2 pts ≥80%, 1 pt ≥65%, 0 pts below.
2. ATR coverage2 Long strike > 2.0 × ATR(14) below current price The max-loss level requires a historically extreme move just to reach it. At 2× ATR the stock would have to make back-to-back near-record daily moves for 30 days. 2 pts ≥2.5×, 1 pt ≥2.0×.
3. Support layers2 ≥ 2 independent support levels below the long strike Count major structures sitting between the long strike and the current price, or below it: 50-day SMA, 100-day SMA, 200-day SMA, 52-week low, rolling 20-day low, prior significant pivot low. Each is a price magnet and bounce zone. 2 pts ≥3 layers, 1 pt ≥2.
4. Volume capitulation1 Current volume ≥ 1.5× the 20-day average A spike in volume on a down day signals panic exhaustion rather than orderly distribution. Heavy sell volume at an already oversold level is one of the most reliable mean-reversion precursors.
5. Reversal candle1 Lower wick ≥ 2× the candle body on the signal day A hammer, doji, or long-tailed candle shows sellers tried to push lower intraday but buyers stepped in. Combined with the other extreme readings this is price-action confirmation of a panic low.
6. MACD exhaustion1 MACD histogram is negative but has been rising for ≥ 2 bars The trend is still down (histogram negative) but momentum is waning — classic first signal of momentum exhaustion before a mean-reversion bounce, occurring well before a bullish MACD cross.
7. Broad market trend1 SPY close > its 200-day SMA Avoids selling puts on single stocks during a market-wide bear phase, where correlated drawdowns can breach spread widths that would be safe in a healthy tape. The most important risk-off filter.
8. Broad market oversold1 SPY RSI(2) < 25 When the broad market is simultaneously washed out, put premium across single names is at its richest and the setup for a coordinated bounce is strongest. Not required, but a meaningful plus.
9. IV environment1 HV rank ≥ 40 on the signal day (proxy for elevated IV) Entering in a high-vol environment means you are selling richer premium and IV crush adds a second tailwind beyond the directional move. At low HV rank the credit barely compensates for the risk.

Why this specifically guards the long strike

The ATR coverage factor directly asks: "how many historically extreme daily moves would it take to breach my max-loss level?" If the long strike is 2.5× ATR below the current price, a catastrophic breach requires a chain of events more severe than almost anything in the ticker's recorded history — statistically, the stock is far more likely to bounce than to reach that level.

The support layers factor ensures the long strike sits beneath real structural floors rather than in open air. Every moving average and pivot level between the current price and the long strike is a zone where buyers have historically stepped in. The more layers, the more buy orders stand between the stock and your maximum-loss level.

Gap risk: none of these factors eliminate overnight gap risk (earnings, macro events, FDA decisions). The earnings-calendar check in the entry checklist (§4) is mandatory precisely because a single gap through the long strike wipes out every technical floor at once.
Quick worked example — AAPL at $185, balanced profile
  • Entry signal: RSI(2) = 3.1, close < lower BB, z-score = -1.87, close > SMA200 ✓
  • Spread: short put $175 (30Δ), long put $170 (further-from-money leg)
  • ATR(14) = $3.20 → long strike $170 is 4.7× ATR below $185 → 2 pts
  • Support layers below $170: 200-SMA $168, 52-week low $164 → 2 layers → 1 pt (need ≥3 for 2 pts)
  • Volume: 2.1× average → 1 pt
  • Reversal candle: lower wick 1.8× body → 0 pts (just missed)
  • MACD histogram: −0.12 → −0.09 → −0.05 (rising 3 bars) → 1 pt
  • SPY > SMA200 → 1 pt; SPY RSI(2) = 18 → 1 pt; HV rank 62 → 1 pt
  • Backtest: 14 of 17 past signals ended positive at 30 days = 82% → 2 pts
  • Total: 10 / 12 → High-confidence. Enter at full balanced size.

4 — Entry Checklist (all conditions required)

Bullish setup → sell $5-wide bull put spread

  • ✓ Trend filter: close > 200-day SMA — only fade dips in long-term uptrends
  • ✓ RSI(2) < 5 — deep short-term oversold (Connors-style)
  • ✓ Close below the lower Bollinger Band (20, 2.0)
  • ✓ 5-day return z-score < −1.5 vs. its 1-year distribution
  • ✓ IV Rank ≥ 30 — premium is actually rich enough to sell
  • ✓ Liquidity: open interest ≥ 500 at target strikes, bid/ask ≤ $0.15 on each leg, $1-increment strikes
  • ✓ No earnings inside the holding window (next 30 days)

Bearish setup → sell $5-wide bear call spread (mirror)

  • Close < 200-day SMA, RSI(2) > 95, close above upper Bollinger Band, z-score > +1.5, same IV/liquidity/earnings rules

Strike selection

  • Short strike: at the option delta for your profile (10Δ / 30Δ / 50Δ) — for Balanced this usually lands at or just below the lower Bollinger Band after the selloff, i.e., the market has to keep crashing through an already-extreme level for you to lose
  • Long strike: exactly $5 farther out
  • Expiry: 30–45 DTE at entry

5 — Exit & Risk Rules

  • Profit target: buy the spread back at 80% of max profit (e.g., collected $1.65 → close at $0.33). GTC order placed immediately at fill.
  • Stop: close if the spread trades at 2× the credit received (loss ≈ the original credit) or the underlying closes through the short strike — whichever happens first.
  • Time exit: close at 21 DTE or 30 calendar days in the trade, whichever comes first. Never hold into the gamma-risk window.
  • Sizing: max loss (width − credit) ≤ 2% of account per position; max 5 concurrent positions; max 2 per sector.

Worked expected-value example (Balanced profile)

Credit $1.65 on $5 width  |  realized win rate 87%  |  profit take at 80% of credit

Win:   +$1.32 per share  (×100 = +$132 per spread)
Loss:  −$2.20 per share average (stop at 2× credit, padded for gaps)

EV = 0.87 × $132  −  0.13 × $220
   = $114.84 − $28.60
   = +$86 per spread, per ~3-week cycle (before commissions)
Honesty check: the 87% is an estimate conditioned on the full checklist, not a guarantee. Track every trade in this journal; if your rolling 20-trade win rate drops below ~75% on the Balanced profile, stop and re-evaluate — the regime may have changed.

6 — The Scanner (free data, no API key)

Two implementations live in the scanner/ folder of this repo. Neither requires a paid or rate-limited API key.

Option A — Python scanner (primary)

scanner/mean_reversion_scanner.py pulls free daily price history and live option chains from Yahoo Finance's public endpoints via yfinance (no key, no signup), with a Stooq CSV fallback for prices. It screens the universe with the §4 checklist, then builds real $5-wide spread candidates from the live chain for all three profiles, computing credit, max ROI, Black-Scholes POP, and expected value. 

# one-time setup
pip install yfinance pandas numpy

# run the scan (defaults: bullish + bearish, all profiles)
python scanner/mean_reversion_scanner.py

# only balanced-profile bullish setups, save to CSV
python scanner/mean_reversion_scanner.py --side bull --profile balanced --csv out.csv

Option B — thinkorswim (fallback / no Python needed)

scanner/MeanReversionSpreads.ts contains a chart study plus Stock Hacker scan code. thinkScript cannot price spreads inside a scan, so the workflow is: Stock Hacker finds the §4 signal tickers, the chart study labels the suggested short-strike zone and IV Rank, and you build the $5-wide spread from the option chain at your profile's delta.

thinkorswim Stock Hacker scan snippet (bullish) 
# Stock Hacker → Add filter → Study → Custom, paste:
def sma200 = Average(close, 200);
def rsi2   = RSI(price = close, length = 2);
def lowBB  = BollingerBands(length = 20, Num_Dev_Dn = -2.0).LowerBand;
plot scan  = close > sma200 and rsi2 < 5 and close < lowBB;

Full study with bear side, z-score, IV Rank labels, and strike-zone plot is in scanner/MeanReversionSpreads.ts.
